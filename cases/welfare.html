Street to ministry · social welfare

Help in the spring. A bill in the fall.

This page is a story about how public money leaks in welfare and intake. It is not a signed deal. DualisCapax has not recovered these dollars. No household named here is a client.

A parent gets a benefit in April because a worker finally opens the file. In October a letter arrives: you were overpaid. Pay it back. The money already went to rent. The clawback does not put a worker in the building. It just moves a number from one ledger to another and leaves the family with a bill.

That is one household. A city intake desk sees the same file three times because the first counter visit never wrote a note the next shift can find. A provincial desk pays a vendor to scan paper that already exists as a PDF on another drive. A federal desk holds a file while two programs argue about which one owns the person.

What the sheet would show

  • Repeat counter visits on the same file.
  • Clawback letters after a benefit already spent on rent and food.
  • Vendor scans of records the desk already holds.
  • Idle days while two programs argue ownership.

If a desk sat down with DualisCapax, they would bring their own export. That file would stay on their device. We would write one figure they can read out loud. If the figure holds, recovered dollars go back into case time, housing, and the next intake shift — not into a slogan.

The sample rows live on the demonstration sheet. They are invented so you can see the shape. They are not measured savings.
