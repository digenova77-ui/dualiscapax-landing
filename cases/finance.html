The system in between · finance

The dollar left your account. Then it paid a fee to sit.

This page is a story about how public money leaks in payment systems, vendor fees, and books that do not talk to each other. It is not a signed deal. DualisCapax has not recovered these dollars. Crypto here means a lock you can test, not a coin we are selling.

A city pays a processor to move benefits. A school board pays another processor to move payroll. A hospital pays a third to move a supplier invoice. Each hop takes a cut. Some cuts are earned. Some are the cost of a file that had to be keyed twice because the first system and the second system do not share a receipt.

That is the financial system in between the taxpayer and the work: not a villain in a cape, a stack of fees and duplicate entries. When DualisCapax says crypto, it means you can check the lock on that receipt the way you check a store slip. It does not mean a token. It does not mean a share.

What the sheet would show

  • Duplicate keying of the same invoice across two systems.
  • Processor fees on a payment that already had a public receipt.
  • Idle float: money that left a ministry and sat three days in a vendor account.
  • Books that cannot be added because the cost centre codes do not match.

If a finance desk sat down, they would bring their own export. That file would stay on their device. Recovered dollars would go back into the work the fee was supposed to serve — beds, buses, case time, parts — not into another processor.
