Nation · defence and procurement

A ship waits. The spare is in another warehouse.

This page is a story about how public money leaks in defence procurement and the industrial supply chain. It is not a signed deal. DualisCapax has not recovered these dollars. No ministry or contractor named here is a client.

A crew is ready. The part is listed as in stock. The stock is in a building three provinces away because the contract was written against a warehouse code nobody updated. Days go by. Overtime on the dock. A second vendor is paid to “expedite.” The first vendor is still paid for the original line. The taxpayer funds both.

That pattern is not a movie. It is a purchase order, a change order, and a line that never closed. The military complex is also a paperwork complex: specs that balloon, trials that restart, and a spare that exists on two ledgers and on zero shelves.

What the sheet would show

  • Change orders that pay for the same spare twice.
  • Idle crew hours while a part sits in the wrong warehouse.
  • Expedite fees stacked on an original line that never closed.
  • Trials restarted because the spec page and the delivered page do not match.

If a procurement desk sat down, they would bring their own export. That file would stay on their device. Recovered dollars would go back into parts that arrive, hours that are not wasted on a dock, and the next crew that is actually ready — not into a speech about readiness.
