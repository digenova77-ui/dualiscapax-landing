Residual law reward inside the Unity Framework

An onboard commitment is not a tip and not a tribe badge. It is a decision to drive down bleeding cost — residual that burns without a close — and to be rewarded for that decision under one law.

Introduction

The Unity Framework

Unity is the rule that free agents share the same peg. Capacity is measured. Residual is priced. Close is required. No preferred tribe, no private exception, no cost that is allowed to bleed forever because the story sounded good.

In finance language: residual law. In systems language: every decision leaves a load; Unity demands that load be bound to capacity and closed when matched. DualisCapax is the residual-law firm that carries that framework into seats, research, adaptive AI, and engineering surfaces.

Unity of free will means you still choose. The framework does not choose for you. It only refuses the lie that choice can ignore residual forever.

What “bleeding cost” means here

Bleeding cost is residual without a close: spend that continues, capacity that is not measured against it, and books that cannot say what remains. Most organizations live inside that bleed — vendor stacks, open research with no ledger, AI runs with no residual line.

An onboard commitment is a public refusal of that pattern. You lock capacity. You accept that residual will be measured. You agree that unused burn is not free simply because it was enthusiastic.

How the commitment is rewarded

Reward inside Unity is not points and not equity-by-default. It is residual order — recognition and continuity that track the ledger of who closed bleed early, at what scale, and under the same peg as everyone else.

  • Lock residual capacity — prepaid seat against measured capacity, not a subscription that pretends cost is optional.
  • Accept the peg — residual burn is recorded against that capacity until spent or released under seat terms.
  • Drive bleed down — the seat exists to make bleeding cost visible and stoppable, not to hide it in marketing tiers.
  • Earn residual order — earlier and clearer locks receive founding recognition, early residual pricing when seats open, and continuity of access for the term — same product quality as later entrants, better place in the order of close.
Worked example · when seats open

Actor A locks a founding-threshold residual in the first window. Bleed on their side of the book is now pegged. Under Unity they are eligible for the milestone plaque (if they authorize the name), early residual pricing, and a seat that does not get a softer product later — only an earlier close in residual order.

Actor B waits until the window moves. Same peg, same research and adaptive surfaces, same law — later residual order, no founding plaque claim, standard open pricing.

Neither receives equity by default, a tribe preference, or a guaranteed financial return. The reward is the structure of residual law applied fairly: early commitment against bleed is remembered in the ledger.

What Unity rewards — and what it does not

Rewarded

  • Commitment that makes bleeding cost measurable and closeable
  • Early residual lock above the founding threshold (plaque + early order)
  • Scale of capacity funded — larger residual, stronger continuity when rules publish
  • Pledged residual that advances the public singularity model (horizon pulled earlier)

Not rewarded

  • Noise without a close
  • Preferred tribes, private exceptions, or “special” residual
  • Claims of securities, dividends, or guaranteed return from a seat
  • Access sold before pricing and IP protection are locked

Same law for corp and grassroots

A major corporation that pre-funds constrained research capacity under residual cost pegging and a small team that locks a modest seat are under the same Unity rule. The books differ in size; the peg does not. That is the framework: free will to enter, one residual law once inside.
