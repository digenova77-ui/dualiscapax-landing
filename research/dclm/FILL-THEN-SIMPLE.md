# Fill-then-simple — launch vs stand
2026-08-26 22:42 EDT

Once every meter the tweak uses has a cite, the DCLM act is the same as a lemonade stand: smash, walk-back, dated next_cut.
Getting those meters is not the same act as the stand.

## Instances (do not merge)

Elon is not a legal name we model as one blob.
Tesla (NYSE issuer, 10-K pile) ≠ SpaceX (Space Exploration Technologies Corp.) ≠ other ventures.
Private investor books that were never filed are not P1. Dualis does not own them unless granted.

## What public SpaceX paper can fill tonight (pattern)

Offering / FWP-class summaries in 2026 show consolidated revenue and cost, and segment cuts (Space / Connectivity / AI), plus named Starship R&D and capex lines.
Example vintage cells (cite the filing, do not treat as Dualis audit): 2025 revenue ~$18.7B; Space revenue ~$4.1B with material Starship R&D; Connectivity ~$11.4B. That is C5 P1 at **segment** altitude.
It is not propellant per flight, not scrap per anomaly, not pad-hour cost. Those stay empty until a note or a grant holds them.

## Grade

A coarse public income statement = C, maybe B if segments walk back.
Per-launch residual = still missing. Do not tell the street Dualis has a launch tweak as tight as a camp snack line.

## Argument we accept

If the 101st document and every prospectus that is *actually public* are harvested into the named instance, the chair becomes simple.
We have not finished that harvest. Nasdaq/NYSE is the wrong shelf for years of SpaceX. The right shelf is the filings and offering memos that exist — then grants for the rest.
