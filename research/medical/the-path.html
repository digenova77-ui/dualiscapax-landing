Before awareness

The path

He did not start healthy and pick a cause. The disease was already in the bone. The leg was already gone. The ward had already shown him children in the same fire. Then he chose more friction.

March 1977 · 18 years old

Pain in the right knee that he treated like basketball, then like a car bump. He could barely move after a training run. Rolly drove him to Royal Columbian. Dr. Michael Piper read the X-ray. Bone scan, 4 March: osteogenic sarcoma. Three days later — 9 March — the right leg came off 15 cm above the knee. That was the treatment then. There was not a spare-the-limb option waiting on a form.

Betty remembered the number they were given: twenty to fifty percent chance he would live. Not a slogan. A family in a room.

The night before the cut, his coach brought a Runner’s World piece on Dick Traum, an amputee who finished New York. Terry told the nurse in the morning: someday I’m going to do something like that.

His brother asked why it had to be him. Terry said: why not me.

The ward

Sixteen months of methotrexate and adriamycin at the B.C. cancer clinic. Hair gone. Sick every three weeks. He saw patients younger than he was. That is where the run was born — not on a poster. In a hallway of other people’s leftover.

He learned the limb. Golf with his father in a month. Wheelchair basketball. A 27 km race in Prince George. Last place. People cheered anyway.

The letter · 15 October 1979

“I’m not a dreamer, and I’m not saying this will initiate any kind of definitive answer or cure to cancer, but I believe in miracles. I have to.”

Written to the Canadian Cancer Society. He had already trained thousands of kilometres on a hop-step prosthesis. He asked for a dollar from every Canadian. He did not claim Dualis math. He claimed a miracle because the alternative was to watch the ward stay the ward.

12 April 1980 — 1 September 1980

St. John’s. Artificial foot in the Atlantic. Then a marathon a day on pavement. Hop. Step. Hop. Weather. Pain. 143 days. 5,373 km. Six provinces.

Outside Thunder Bay the cancer was in the lungs. He stopped. He said he would do his very best. He promised he would not give up. He went home. Ten months later — 28 June 1981 — Royal Columbian again. One month short of twenty-three.

The fund crossed a dollar for every Canadian then living. The Foundation kept the mile.

What this is not

Not Dualis treatment. Not Dualis owning the will. Not a first-search paragraph that starts at St. John’s and skips the knee. The infection was first. The choice was second. That order is the feeling.
