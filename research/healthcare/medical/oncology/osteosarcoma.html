Where research stands

Osteosarcoma is the most common primary bone sarcoma in adolescents and young adults. Since the 1970s–80s the backbone has remained neoadjuvant / adjuvant MAP (high-dose methotrexate, doxorubicin, cisplatin) plus surgical resection of primary disease and resectable metastases. Localized disease 5-year overall survival is commonly cited in the ~65–70% range in modern series; metastatic disease at diagnosis remains far worse (often ~20–30% EFS in resectable metastatic cohorts; lower when unresectable).

Intensification after poor histologic response (e.g. MAPIE in EURAMOS-1) did not improve outcomes enough to displace MAP as the default North American / European standard for localized disease. Adult-oriented trials continue to test whether high-dose methotrexate can be moderated (e.g. doxorubicin/cisplatin vs MAP in older adults).

Public residual support

  • NCI PDQ — Osteosarcoma and UPS of Bone Treatment: survival improvement 1975–2020; MAP backbone; metastatic EFS bands.
  • MAP meta-analyses (2022–2024 class): MAP remains preferable on balance of outcomes and AE vs many alternatives; HD-MTX debates continue in adults.
  • EURAMOS-era lesson: adding IE for poor responders did not rewrite the standard for localized disease.
  • American Cancer Society / Mayo Clinic public survival framing: stage and resectability dominate interpretation — statistics describe groups, not individuals.
