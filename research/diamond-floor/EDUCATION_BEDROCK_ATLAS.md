# Diamond Floor: Education Bedrock Atlas

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** research taxonomy / synthesis candidate; not a claim of exhaustive coverage or independently verified truth.  
**Scope:** a navigable map from foundational assumptions and methods (bedrock) through disciplines and subfields to applications, measurements, and open questions.

## Why this exists

The aim is to make knowledge inspectable across boundaries rather than treating school subjects as isolated boxes. Every branch should be traceable down to its assumptions and methods, and upward to the claims, tools, and decisions it supports. Connections are hypotheses until evidence supports them.

This atlas is a starting scaffold for a source-backed, versioned knowledge graph. It is not a replacement for domain experts, original literature, empirical work, or formal curricula.

## The vertical stack

1. **Bedrock — admissibility:** What counts as a claim, a definition, a proof, an observation, an interpretation, or an ethical constraint?
2. **Formal foundations — structure:** Logic, mathematics, probability, computation, measurement theory, language, and systems concepts.
3. **Methods — warrant:** Deduction, induction, abduction, experiment, observation, modeling, simulation, historical interpretation, comparative analysis, and design.
4. **Core disciplines — explanation:** Fields that develop specialized concepts, evidence standards, instruments, and explanatory models.
5. **Subdisciplines — resolution:** Narrower fields with explicit scope and methods.
6. **Applications — intervention:** Engineering, medicine, policy, education, art, operations, and other changes in the world.
7. **Audit and feedback — correction:** Replication, measurement validation, peer criticism, impact assessment, incident analysis, and revision.

The stack is not a universal hierarchy of importance. In practice, evidence and questions move in both directions: an application can expose a missing assumption at bedrock.

## Cross-disciplinary bedrock rules

- Separate a definition from a fact claim, and a model from the thing modeled.
- Separate data acquisition, data cleaning, inference, interpretation, and decision.
- Record units, reference frames, calibration, uncertainty, provenance, and missingness.
- A proof establishes a conclusion relative to stated axioms and inference rules; it does not by itself establish that the axioms describe the physical world.
- A successful experiment supports a bounded claim under its conditions; it does not establish universal truth.
- Correlation is not, by itself, causation.
- A simulation establishes what follows from its assumptions and implementation; validation against the world is a separate task.
- Historical, linguistic, artistic, and ethical claims require methods suited to their objects; not every meaningful question reduces to a numerical metric.
- Human-subject research requires consent, privacy protection, risk review, and respect for communities.
- A status label, citation, hash, signature, or model output is not a substitute for checking the underlying evidence.
- Contradictory evidence must remain visible; do not average away a real disagreement without explaining why.
- Unknown, unmeasured, inaccessible, and false are distinct states.

## Discipline atlas

Each domain is summarized as: **bedrock question → core methods → major branches → applied outputs → unresolved frontier**.

### 1. Logic, mathematics, and formal systems
- **Bedrock question:** What follows from definitions, axioms, and valid inference?
- **Core methods:** proof, abstraction, construction, counterexample, formalization, numerical analysis.
- **Branches:** logic and foundations; algebra; number theory; geometry and topology; analysis; probability and statistics; combinatorics; optimization; dynamical systems; information theory; category and type theory; applied mathematics.
- **Applied outputs:** proofs, algorithms, quantitative models, uncertainty estimates, optimization and control.
- **Frontiers:** foundations and consistency; complexity limits; formal verification at scale; mathematical models for multiscale and emergent systems.
- **Evidence gate:** explicit assumptions, reproducible derivation, independently checked proof or calculation.

### 2. Computer science and information
- **Bedrock question:** What can be represented, computed, communicated, stored, and verified?
- **Core methods:** algorithms, semantics, complexity analysis, systems measurement, testing, formal methods.
- **Branches:** theory of computation; algorithms and data structures; programming languages; operating systems; distributed systems; databases; networking; security and privacy; AI and machine learning; human-computer interaction; graphics; robotics; scientific computing; information retrieval.
- **Applied outputs:** software, protocols, data systems, verified computations, interactive tools.
- **Frontiers:** dependable AI; distributed trust; interpretability; resilient long-term data preservation; energy and resource-aware computation.
- **Evidence gate:** versioned code and dependencies, tests, threat model, reproducible build, observed runtime behavior, explicit limits.

### 3. Physics
- **Bedrock question:** What regularities govern matter, energy, space, time, fields, and interactions?
- **Core methods:** measurement, mathematical modeling, controlled experiment, observation, dimensional analysis, uncertainty propagation.
- **Branches:** classical mechanics; electromagnetism; thermodynamics and statistical mechanics; quantum physics; relativity; optics; atomic and molecular physics; nuclear and particle physics; condensed matter; plasma physics; acoustics; biophysics; geophysics.
- **Applied outputs:** instruments, energy technologies, materials, communications, physical predictions.
- **Frontiers:** quantum gravity; dark matter and dark energy; nonequilibrium systems; foundations of measurement; multiscale prediction.
- **Evidence gate:** calibrated instruments, stated units and conditions, uncertainty budgets, independent replication where feasible.

### 4. Chemistry and materials
- **Bedrock question:** How do composition, structure, bonding, and reactions produce material properties?
- **Core methods:** synthesis, spectroscopy, chromatography, microscopy, thermodynamic and kinetic modeling.
- **Branches:** organic; inorganic; physical; analytical; theoretical; electrochemistry; biochemistry; polymer science; materials chemistry; surface chemistry; atmospheric chemistry.
- **Applied outputs:** medicines, catalysts, batteries, semiconductors, polymers, environmental remediation.
- **Frontiers:** predictive synthesis; sustainable materials; catalysis; degradation and circularity; chemistry under extreme conditions.
- **Evidence gate:** sample provenance, instrument calibration, methods and conditions, uncertainty, independent characterization.

### 5. Biology and life sciences
- **Bedrock question:** How do living systems originate, maintain themselves, vary, reproduce, evolve, and interact?
- **Core methods:** observation, controlled experiments, field studies, sequencing, imaging, statistics, phylogenetic inference.
- **Branches:** molecular and cell biology; genetics and genomics; evolution; ecology; microbiology; botany; zoology; physiology; developmental biology; neuroscience; immunology; systems biology; synthetic biology.
- **Applied outputs:** conservation, agriculture, biotechnology, diagnostics, therapeutics.
- **Frontiers:** origin of life; ecosystem resilience; complex genotype-phenotype relations; multiscale regulation; reproducibility.
- **Evidence gate:** controls, sample size and selection, contamination checks, preregistration where appropriate, ethical oversight, replication.

### 6. Earth, ocean, climate, and environmental sciences
- **Bedrock question:** How does the coupled Earth system change across space and time, and how can those changes be observed?
- **Core methods:** field sampling, remote sensing, geophysical instruments, laboratory analysis, inverse problems, climate and earth-system modeling.
- **Branches:** geology; seismology; geodesy; geomorphology; hydrology; oceanography; atmospheric science; meteorology; climatology; glaciology; soil science; paleontology; environmental chemistry; biogeochemistry; natural hazards.
- **Applied outputs:** hazard assessment, water and land management, climate projections, resource stewardship, environmental monitoring.
- **Frontiers:** subsurface structure and dynamics; coupled feedbacks; extreme-event attribution; deep-time reconstruction; sensor fusion and observability.
- **Evidence gate:** sensor calibration, station/location metadata, temporal coverage, uncertainty, model-data comparison, independent datasets.
- **Boundary:** Earth's physical systems record and transform signals, but claims that they contain recoverable information about arbitrary events or consciousness require a specific mechanism, measurable hypothesis, and tests that could falsify it.

### 7. Astronomy and space science
- **Bedrock question:** What can be inferred about celestial objects and the universe from light, particles, fields, and gravitational effects?
- **Core methods:** telescopic observation, spectroscopy, astrometry, timing, detector calibration, statistical inference, simulation.
- **Branches:** solar physics; planetary science; stellar astrophysics; galactic astronomy; cosmology; exoplanets; high-energy astrophysics; gravitational-wave astronomy; space weather; astrobiology.
- **Applied outputs:** navigation, space missions, solar monitoring, fundamental cosmological constraints.
- **Frontiers:** origin and evolution of the universe; planetary habitability; dark components; multi-messenger inference.
- **Evidence gate:** calibration, selection effects, uncertainty, reproducible reduction pipelines, independent instruments where available.

### 8. Engineering and design
- **Bedrock question:** How can a system be designed to meet a need under constraints, with acceptable risk?
- **Core methods:** requirements, modeling, prototyping, verification, validation, safety analysis, lifecycle assessment.
- **Branches:** mechanical; electrical; civil; chemical; aerospace; biomedical; environmental; industrial; systems; control; software; materials; energy; manufacturing; human factors.
- **Applied outputs:** infrastructure, machines, software, products, controls, resilient services.
- **Frontiers:** safety of complex autonomous systems; circular design; human-machine coordination; verification under uncertainty.
- **Evidence gate:** traceable requirements, independent tests, failure analysis, lifecycle and safety evidence, rollback or containment plan.

### 9. Medicine, public health, and health professions
- **Bedrock question:** What improves health and reduces harm for individuals and populations?
- **Core methods:** clinical examination, diagnostic validation, randomized and observational studies, epidemiology, evidence synthesis, surveillance.
- **Branches:** anatomy; physiology; pathology; pharmacology; clinical specialties; nursing; dentistry; rehabilitation; mental health; epidemiology; biostatistics; health systems; health economics; implementation science.
- **Applied outputs:** prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, public-health policy.
- **Frontiers:** personalized care; complex chronic disease; health inequities; translation from evidence to practice.
- **Evidence gate:** clinical validity, meaningful outcomes, safety monitoring, consent, privacy, ethics review, external replication.

### 10. Psychology, cognition, and neuroscience
- **Bedrock question:** How do organisms perceive, learn, remember, decide, feel, and act?
- **Core methods:** behavioral experiments, psychometrics, neuroimaging, electrophysiology, computational models, longitudinal and cross-cultural studies.
- **Branches:** cognitive; developmental; social; clinical; educational; personality; behavioral; neuropsychology; affective neuroscience; computational neuroscience; perception and action.
- **Applied outputs:** learning design, mental-health care, accessibility, human factors, behavioral interventions.
- **Frontiers:** consciousness theories and tests; cognition across scales; measurement validity; generalization across cultures and individuals.
- **Evidence gate:** operational definitions, validated instruments, confounds, preregistration where appropriate, replication, privacy and consent.

### 11. Social sciences and society
- **Bedrock question:** How do people, groups, institutions, norms, and environments interact?
- **Core methods:** surveys, experiments, ethnography, interviews, administrative data analysis, causal inference, comparative and historical methods.
- **Branches:** sociology; anthropology; political science; human geography; demography; communication; criminology; international relations; social psychology; science and technology studies.
- **Applied outputs:** institutional design, social policy, community research, program evaluation.
- **Frontiers:** polarization; collective behavior; digital social systems; causal inference in complex settings; cross-cultural generalization.
- **Evidence gate:** sampling and representation, context, measurement validity, confounding, consent, privacy, researcher positionality.

### 12. Economics, finance, and management
- **Bedrock question:** How are scarce resources allocated, incentives formed, value produced, and organizations coordinated?
- **Core methods:** formal models, econometrics, experiments, accounting, causal analysis, scenario and risk analysis.
- **Branches:** microeconomics; macroeconomics; behavioral; development; labor; public; environmental; financial economics; accounting; operations research; strategy; organizational behavior; entrepreneurship.
- **Applied outputs:** policy analysis, resource allocation, risk management, organizational decisions.
- **Frontiers:** systemic risk; distributional effects; climate and ecological constraints; institutional incentives; limits of prediction.
- **Evidence gate:** declared assumptions, data lineage, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty, conflicts of interest, distinction between descriptive and normative claims.

### 13. Humanities: history, philosophy, languages, and religion
- **Bedrock question:** How do humans create meaning, preserve memory, reason about values, and interpret texts and traditions?
- **Core methods:** close reading, archival criticism, philology, argument analysis, source comparison, oral history, hermeneutics.
- **Branches:** history; philosophy; ethics; epistemology; metaphysics; political philosophy; linguistics; literature; classics; religious studies; intellectual history; cultural studies.
- **Applied outputs:** interpretation, ethical reasoning, historical understanding, civic discourse, preservation of cultural records.
- **Frontiers:** contested memory; translation across conceptual worlds; digital humanities; plural ethical frameworks; historical silence and bias.
- **Evidence gate:** provenance and context of sources, distinction between quotation and interpretation, counterevidence, transparent argument.

### 14. Arts, music, media, and creative practice
- **Bedrock question:** How do forms, materials, performance, and symbols create experience and communicate meaning?
- **Core methods:** making, rehearsal, critique, formal analysis, ethnography, archival study, audience research.
- **Branches:** visual arts; music; theatre; dance; film; photography; architecture; design; creative writing; media arts; craft; game design.
- **Applied outputs:** artworks, performances, communication, cultural memory, experience design.
- **Frontiers:** generative tools and authorship; preservation of ephemeral work; access and participation; cross-cultural interpretation.
- **Evidence gate:** document process and context, distinguish aesthetic judgment from empirical claims, respect authorship and cultural rights.

### 15. Law, governance, and ethics
- **Bedrock question:** What rules and procedures legitimately govern conduct, resolve conflict, allocate authority, and constrain power?
- **Core methods:** statutory and case analysis, interpretation, precedent, comparative law, policy analysis, ethical argument, impact assessment.
- **Branches:** constitutional; administrative; criminal; civil; contract; property; intellectual property; international; environmental; privacy; technology law; jurisprudence; bioethics.
- **Applied outputs:** rules, institutions, remedies, accountability, governance frameworks.
- **Frontiers:** governance of autonomous systems; cross-border data; intergenerational justice; enforcement under concentrated power.
- **Evidence gate:** jurisdiction and effective date, authoritative sources, due process, traceable authority, conflict-of-interest disclosure.

### 16. Education and learning sciences
- **Bedrock question:** How do people learn, how can learning be supported, and how can learning claims be evaluated fairly?
- **Core methods:** learning theory, classroom and field studies, assessment design, longitudinal research, design-based research, program evaluation.
- **Branches:** pedagogy; curriculum; instructional design; assessment; educational psychology; special education; adult learning; early childhood; higher education; vocational education; educational technology; policy and leadership.
- **Applied outputs:** curricula, teaching practice, learning resources, assessment, accessible learning environments.
- **Frontiers:** transfer of learning; equitable measurement; motivation; adaptive systems; evidence for technology-enhanced learning.
- **Evidence gate:** clear learning outcomes, validity and reliability, accessibility, fairness, privacy, evaluation beyond engagement metrics.

### 17. Agriculture, food, and ecological stewardship
- **Bedrock question:** How can food systems nourish people while sustaining soil, water, biodiversity, and livelihoods?
- **Core methods:** field trials, soil and crop measurements, food chemistry, supply-chain analysis, ecological modeling.
- **Branches:** agronomy; animal science; horticulture; forestry; fisheries; food science; nutrition; agroecology; food safety; agricultural economics.
- **Applied outputs:** resilient food production, safe processing, conservation, sustainable supply chains.
- **Frontiers:** soil carbon and uncertainty; climate adaptation; biodiversity; waste reduction; equitable access.
- **Evidence gate:** local conditions, multi-season data, safety controls, lifecycle impacts, transparent trade-offs.

### 18. Interdisciplinary and emerging fields
- **Bedrock question:** What questions cannot be adequately answered by one discipline alone?
- **Branches:** complex systems; network science; cognitive science; bioinformatics; computational social science; sustainability science; science of science; human-computer interaction; planetary health; digital humanities; systems biology; quantum information.
- **Applied outputs:** integrated models and tools that connect evidence across domains.
- **Frontiers:** integrating incompatible scales, standards, causal models, uncertainty, and values without flattening them.
- **Evidence gate:** each contributing discipline's methods remain visible; integration assumptions and cross-domain translations are explicit.

## Crosswalk: how to move down and up

For every concept, ask both directions:

**Downward to bedrock**
- What exactly is being claimed?
- Which definitions and assumptions does it depend on?
- What observations or proofs could support or contradict it?
- Which instrument, method, dataset, or source generated the evidence?
- What are the uncertainty, bias, and scope limits?
- What would make the claim fail?

**Upward to application**
- Which decisions or interventions rely on this claim?
- What must be true for the result to transfer to another context?
- Who bears the risks and receives the benefits?
- What measurements would reveal harm or failure?
- Who has authority to accept, reject, or deploy the result?
- How will the outcome be monitored and corrected?

## Extraction record schema

Every extracted concept should become a record with:
- concept_id: stable identifier, independent of display name.
- label and aliases: preserve terminology and synonyms.
- discipline and subdiscipline.
- definition: concise and source-attributed.
- claim_type: definition, theorem, observation, model, interpretation, normative claim, or hypothesis.
- bedrock_dependencies: definitions, axioms, methods, or concepts required.
- upward_links: branches, applications, or decisions that depend on it.
- evidence: citations or retained artifacts with source, date, scope, and digest where appropriate.
- verification_state: unreviewed, needs-review, partial, passed-bounded-check, blocked, or failed.
- uncertainty_and_counterevidence.
- rights_and_ethics.
- revision_history and reviewer.

Do not treat this schema as evidence by itself. Populated records need sources and review.

## Current gaps and next extraction phases

This first atlas is broad but not exhaustive. It intentionally does not pretend that every subfield, tradition, language, or contested theory has been captured. Next phases should:

1. Convert this human-readable atlas into a machine-readable, versioned graph with stable IDs and explicit edge types.
2. Add authoritative textbooks, standards, primary papers, data repositories, and institutional curricula for each discipline.
3. Record multiple schools of thought and disagreements rather than forcing a single consensus where none exists.
4. Build test fixtures that reject unsupported citations, circular provenance, missing scope, fabricated verification, and silent status promotion.
5. Add independent domain-expert review and track what was reviewed, by whom, against which version.
6. Keep descriptive findings separate from ethical and policy choices.
7. Make the full export portable and independently verifiable; do not require a proprietary viewer.

**Success criterion:** a learner or reviewer can trace a specific claim from an application to its supporting evidence and methods, then down to its assumptions—and can also move upward from foundational concepts to see where they are used, with uncertainty and disagreement preserved at every step.
