Residual unit

What is metered here is the cost of irreversible work: capacity spent when information is erased, when fidelity is forced higher, and when process rework is repeated. Local engineering floors (your link budget, your PA backoff, your token budget) are real for the ship — they are not automatically the ultimate floor of physics.

P1 — Literature-forward residual

Landauer bound. Erasing one bit of information has a minimum average heat cost of order kBT ln 2 at temperature T. Real digital machines operate far above that floor because of noise margins, speed, and interconnect — but the bound still forbids treating irreversible residual as free.

Rate–distortion. For a source under a distortion budget D, the minimum average rate R(D) is the least mutual information needed to keep average error ≤ D. For a Gaussian source under mean squared error, R(D) = ½ log2(σ²/D) when D ≤ σ². Higher fidelity requires more capacity.

Waterfilling. Parallel resources (channels or transform coefficients) should receive power or rate only where the waterline still clears noise or residual variance. Dead channels and drowned coefficients should not consume the budget.

Closed measure. Stochastic thermodynamics (fluctuation theorems, Crooks, bidirectional free-energy estimators such as BAR/MBAR) encodes the same honesty: path costs are real; rare optimistic paths do not cancel the ledger; equilibrium claims need a closed comparison of ensembles.

Edge systems. OFDM, PAPR, SC-FDMA, mmWave beams, and RIS designs are all edge residual problems: you engineer the gap (peaks, path loss, blockage) instead of pretending the channel is free.
