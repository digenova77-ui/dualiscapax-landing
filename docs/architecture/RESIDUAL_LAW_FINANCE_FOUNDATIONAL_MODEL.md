# Residual Law Finance — Foundational Model

**Status:** Proposed model for discovery and testing; not legal advice and not an implemented financial product.

## Purpose

Residual Law Finance is a proposed real-world application of Trinity Law: identify, evidence, quantify, validate, and where legally permitted recover value that is lost, withheld, misallocated, under-recovered, or left unrealized.

Its first priority should be public money, where transparent and independently reviewable processes can create public value. Expansion to corporate or other institutional settings should follow evidence from bounded pilots.

## Relationship to the foundation

- **Mathematical-philosophical foundation:** derive decisions from explicit rules, consistent reasoning, evidence, and stated limits.
- **Trinity Law:** govern authorization, permitted scope, evidence requirements, accountability, review, and remedies.
- **Software architecture:** implement those rules as testable, auditable workflows without requiring one vendor or permanent central operator.
- **Residual Law Finance:** apply the framework to financial claims, discrepancies, recovery opportunities, and verified outcomes.

The financial application must not be used to redefine the underlying law to favor a particular claimant, operator, fee model, or outcome.

## Core workflow

1. **Mandate:** record who requested the review, the legal/contractual basis, permitted data, jurisdiction, and limits.
2. **Discover:** identify a candidate discrepancy or recovery opportunity without treating an anomaly as proof of wrongdoing.
3. **Evidence:** preserve source provenance, timestamps, calculations, applicable rule versions, and known gaps.
4. **Quantify:** calculate a range or amount using disclosed assumptions and reproducible methods.
5. **Validate independently:** a reviewer separate from the claimant and fee beneficiary verifies the calculation and supporting evidence.
6. **Determine eligibility:** identify lawful grounds, limitation periods, defenses, due-process rights, and whether recovery is actually permitted.
7. **Authorize action:** an appropriately empowered human or lawful institution approves consequential steps where required.
8. **Resolve and recover:** pursue the approved process; distinguish a proposed claim, accepted claim, amount ordered, amount collected, and net realized recovery.
9. **Settle fees:** if contractually permitted, calculate compensation from a predefined, auditable basis and only after the qualifying result is independently verified.
10. **Review and remedy:** retain a challenge path, correction process, appeal history, and controls against double counting or duplicate claims.

## Financial definitions

Never conflate these values:

- **Potential opportunity:** a hypothesis requiring investigation.
- **Supported claim:** a claim with sufficient documented evidence to proceed to review.
- **Recognized entitlement:** an amount established under the applicable contract or legal process.
- **Gross recovered amount:** funds actually received or otherwise realized under a defined accounting rule.
- **Net public benefit:** verified benefit less fees, operating costs, remediation costs, and other relevant costs, with the calculation disclosed.
- **Disputed or reversed amount:** an amount challenged, returned, cancelled, or later found not to qualify.

A model estimate is not money recovered. A ledger entry is not proof that a source claim is true. A smart contract cannot independently determine the meaning of law or resolve every dispute about off-chain facts.

## Anti-corruption controls

- Fix evaluation criteria and fee rules before reviewing individual cases.
- Disclose beneficial interests and conflicts of interest.
- Separate discovery, validation, authorization, execution, and fee approval when stakes justify it.
- Log policy changes and exceptions with reasons and accountable identities.
- Prohibit the same party from unilaterally inventing a claim, validating it, authorizing its own payment, and certifying its own success.
- Provide independent review, due process, privacy safeguards, and a route to contest errors.
- Audit for duplicate claims, manipulated baselines, selective reporting, inflated estimates, and incentives to pursue high-value cases while neglecting fairness.
- Make no guarantee of recovery; report unsuccessful cases and false positives as well as successes.

## Smallest pilot

Choose one narrow, authorized public-finance workflow—for example, duplicate invoice detection or verification of an already-identified receivable. Use read-only data initially, a fixed evaluation period, a documented baseline, independent reviewers, and no automated transfer of funds.

Measure at minimum:

- precision of flagged cases and false-positive rate;
- independently verified recoveries actually realized;
- net benefit after all costs and fees;
- time from detection to resolution;
- duplicate claims and double counting;
- appeal, reversal, and correction rates;
- distributional effects and privacy incidents.

Only expand authority or automate consequential actions after the pilot passes predefined acceptance criteria and independent review.

## Decision and compensation rule

Any recovery-linked fee must be disclosed in advance, legally permissible, contractually defined, independently auditable, and based on a clearly defined realized outcome—not on an AI estimate or an unverified claim. Conflicts of interest must be managed, and the public or client must retain the right to dispute the calculation.

## Open questions that must be resolved before deployment

1. Which jurisdiction and legal authority govern the first pilot?
2. Who may lawfully provide and process the data?
3. What exact category of financial loss or entitlement is in scope?
4. What constitutes independent verification and realized recovery?
5. Who can authorize recovery action and resolve disputes?
6. How are privacy, retention, privilege, limitation periods, and due process protected?
7. Which costs and counterfactuals are included in net-benefit calculations?
8. What conditions prohibit fees or require repayment of fees?

## Success criterion

A pilot succeeds only when an independent reviewer can reproduce the result from permitted source evidence, confirm that the process was authorized and fair, distinguish estimated from realized value, and verify the net benefit after costs. Anything less is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated recovery.
