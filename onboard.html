What you are locking into

Capital placed here is allocated against residual capacity: research, adaptive AI runs, and financial structure that does not float free of cost. The peg is simple — residual burn is measured; unused capacity stays on the book until it is spent or released under the terms of the seat.

  • Prepaid residual seat (capacity, not equity by default)
  • Cost pegged to measured residual, not marketing tiers
  • Clear line between operating burn and locked reserve

Corporate frame

For major corporations, residual cost pegging is a way to pre-fund constrained technical and research capacity with a traceable cost basis. Depending on jurisdiction and structure, prepaid capacity and qualified research spend can sit in familiar tax and accounting lanes — R&D treatment, prepaid expense recognition, or structured service agreements — when counsel and auditors agree the facts support it.

  • Budget residual as a known cost line, not an open-ended vendor bleed
  • Align spend with documented research and adaptive systems work
  • Keep the residual ledger inspectable for internal control

This is not tax advice. Corporate treatment depends on how the seat is contracted, where the entity sits, and what the capacity is actually used for.

Grassroots, bottom up

The same peg works small. Individuals and small teams can lock modest residual seats, share pooled capacity, and climb the same cost structure without needing a corporate treasury. Bottom-up seats still buy measured residual — not a softer product — so grassroots access stays honest to the same ledger major corps read.

  • Small seats still clear against residual cost, not charity tiers
  • Pooling can aggregate capacity without erasing individual residual
  • No preferred tribe: the peg does not care who funds the seat

What happens next

This page is the brief. Settlement rails (fiat and crypto) attach when the seat is issued. Until then, the residual cost peg is the contract language: you fund capacity; residual is priced; nothing is free that still burns.
